# Ancient Greek Lyre Mode Characteristics Summary

Based on chord progression analysis for 7-string lyre tunings.

---

## DORIOS (H-W-W-W-H-W-W)
*Ancient Dorios - Modern Phrygian (E-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** E F G A B C D
**Tonal Center:** Minor (i)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is DIMINISHED (v°)** - This creates a unique harmonic challenge
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (i, II, III)
- The diminished V chord gives progressions an unstable, modal quality
- V-I cadence complexity: 134.26 (moderate, due to diminished V)

### Best Progressions
1. **v° - i** (V-I): Despite diminished V, still ranks strongest resolution
2. **v° - VI**: Deceptive cadence works surprisingly well
3. **i - v°**: Creates tension effectively

### Musical Character
- Dark, mysterious quality due to minor tonic and diminished dominant
- Well-suited for modal harmony rather than traditional tonal progressions
- The diminished V prevents strong authentic cadences
- Excellent for music that avoids strong resolutions

### Practical Notes
- **Challenge:** Diminished V chord adds inherent dissonance to any progression using it
- **Strength:** The modal ambiguity can be musically interesting
- **Best for:** Modal compositions, atmospheric music, avoiding functional harmony

## PHRYGIOS (W-H-W-W-W-H-W)
*Ancient Phrygios - Modern Dorian (D-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** D E F G A B C
**Tonal Center:** Minor (i)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is MINOR (v)** - Weak leading tone resolution
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (i, ii, III)
- Minor V creates modal rather than tonal sound
- V-I cadence complexity: 138.56 (weaker due to minor V)

### Best Progressions
1. **V - vi°**: Deceptive to diminished vi (132.51 - actually strongest!)
2. **IV - i**: Plagal cadence (133.75)
3. **v - i**: Authentic cadence (138.56)

### Musical Character
- Bright minor sound with major IV chord available
- The minor V softens cadential resolution
- Creates a folk-like, modal atmosphere
- The Phrygian half-step (b2) adds characteristic color when using ii chord

### Practical Notes
- **Challenge:** V-I resolution lacks the strength of major V
- **Strength:** Major IV chord available for strong plagal cadences
- **Best for:** Folk music, modal compositions, softer resolutions

## LYDIOS (W-W-H-W-W-W-H)
*Ancient Lydios - Modern Ionian/Major (C-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** C D E F G A B
**Tonal Center:** MAJOR (I)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is MAJOR (V)** - Strong leading tone! ✓
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (I, ii, iii)
- Full major scale harmony available
- V-I cadence complexity: 134.81 (strong despite inversion)

### Best Progressions
1. **V - vi**: Deceptive cadence (98.82 - exceptional voice leading!)
2. **V - I**: Authentic cadence (134.81)
3. **I - V**: Half cadence (136.01)

### Musical Character
- **THE BEST MODE FOR TRADITIONAL TONAL HARMONY**
- Major tonic with major dominant enables strongest progressions
- All major scale chord progressions theoretically available
- The raised 4th (Lydian characteristic) is present but not problematic

### Practical Notes
- **Strength:** Major V-I provides traditional strong resolution
- **Strength:** Most versatile for Western tonal music
- **Notable:** V-vi has extraordinary voice leading (98.82 complexity!)
- **Best for:** Tonal music, major key compositions, traditional harmony

## MIXOLYDIOS (H-W-W-H-W-W-W)
*Ancient Mixolydios - Modern Locrian (B-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** B C D E F G A
**Tonal Center:** Diminished (i°)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **TONIC IS DIMINISHED (i°)** - Highly unusual!
- **V chord is MAJOR (V)** - But resolves to diminished tonic
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (i°, II, iii)
- V-I cadence complexity: 120.96 (LOWEST OVERALL!)

### Best Progressions
1. **V - i°**: Authentic cadence (120.96 - strongest of all modes!)
2. **i° - V**: Half cadence (122.16)
3. **iv - i°**: Plagal cadence (127.47)

### Musical Character
- **MOST UNUSUAL MODE** - Diminished tonic is extremely rare in Western music
- Despite dim tonic, has the strongest V-I resolution of all modes
- Creates highly unstable, unresolved sound
- The diminished tonic never provides true rest

### Practical Notes
- **Challenge:** Diminished tonic provides no stable resolution
- **Strength:** Paradoxically has the lowest complexity progressions
- **Unique:** Major V to diminished I creates unusual sonority
- **Best for:** Experimental music, creating tension, avoiding resolution

## HYPODORIOS (W-H-W-W-H-W-W)
*Ancient Hypodorios - Modern Aeolian/Natural Minor (A-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** A B C D E F G
**Tonal Center:** Minor (i)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is MINOR (v)** - Natural minor's characteristic weakness
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (i, ii°, III)
- Classic natural minor harmony
- V-I cadence complexity: 138.56 (weak due to minor V)

### Best Progressions
1. **v - i**: Authentic cadence (138.56)
2. **V - VI**: Deceptive cadence (139.40)
3. **i - v** / **iv - i**: Both 139.76

### Musical Character
- **PURE NATURAL MINOR** - The familiar minor scale sound
- Lacks strong V-I resolution (no raised 7th)
- All progressions have similar moderate complexity (~138-140)
- Gives equal weight to multiple cadential options

### Practical Notes
- **Challenge:** Minor V prevents strong dominant resolution
- **Strength:** Familiar natural minor sound
- **Note:** All cadences cluster around 138-140 complexity (very uniform)
- **Best for:** Natural minor compositions, modal minor, folk music

## HYPOLYDIOS (W-W-W-H-W-W-H)
*Ancient Hypolydios - Modern Lydian (F-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** F G A B C D E
**Tonal Center:** MAJOR (I)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is MAJOR (V)** - Strong leading tone! ✓
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (I, II, iii)
- Major scale with raised 4th (Lydian characteristic)
- V-I cadence complexity: 134.81 (same as LYDIOS)

### Best Progressions
1. **V - vi**: Deceptive cadence (98.82 - exceptional!)
2. **V - I**: Authentic cadence (134.81)
3. **I - V**: Half cadence (136.01)

### Musical Character
- **EXCELLENT FOR TONAL HARMONY** - Second best after LYDIOS
- Major tonic and major dominant enable strong progressions
- The raised 4th creates a brighter, more open sound than Ionian
- Identical progression strengths to LYDIOS mode

### Practical Notes
- **Strength:** Major V-I like LYDIOS
- **Strength:** V-vi has the same exceptional voice leading (98.82)
- **Unique:** Raised 4th (B natural in F) creates distinctive color
- **Best for:** Major key music with a bright, open character

---

## HYPOPHRYGIOS (W-W-H-W-W-H-W)
*Ancient Hypophrygios - Modern Mixolydian (G-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** G A B C D E F
**Tonal Center:** MAJOR (I)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is MINOR (v)** - Mixolydian's characteristic b7
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (I, ii, iii°)
- Major tonic but minor dominant (no leading tone)
- V-I cadence complexity: 140.11 (weakest V-I of all modes)

### Best Progressions
1. **V - vi**: Deceptive cadence (104.13)
2. **IV - I**: Plagal cadence (136.01)
3. **v - I**: Authentic cadence (140.11)

### Musical Character
- Major tonic with flatted 7th (F natural in G)
- Minor V prevents strong dominant function
- Creates a relaxed, folk-like major sound
- Strongest cadence is actually the deceptive (V-vi)!

### Practical Notes
- **Challenge:** Minor V is the weakest for V-I resolution
- **Strength:** Major I-IV-I progressions work well
- **Unique:** Mixolydian b7 creates distinctive rock/folk character
- **Best for:** Modal major music, folk, rock, avoiding strong resolutions

## Comparative Summary

### Best Modes for Traditional Tonal Harmony
1. **LYDIOS** - Major I, Major V (ideal)
2. **HYPOLYDIOS** - Major I, Major V (identical to LYDIOS)
3. **MIXOLYDIOS** - Diminished I, Major V (strongest V-I!)

### Best Modes for Modal Harmony
1. **HYPODORIOS** - Natural minor (familiar)
2. **PHRYGIOS** - Dorian sound (bright minor)
3. **DORIOS** - Phrygian sound (dark, exotic)

### Mode Comparison by V Chord Quality

| Mode | Tonic | V Chord | V-I Complexity | Ranking |
|------|-------|---------|----------------|---------|
| MIXOLYDIOS | dim | **MAJOR** | 120.96 | Strongest |
| DORIOS | minor | dim | 134.26 | Moderate |
| LYDIOS | **MAJOR** | **MAJOR** | 134.81 | Strong |
| HYPOLYDIOS | **MAJOR** | **MAJOR** | 134.81 | Strong |
| HYPODORIOS | minor | minor | 138.56 | Weak |
| PHRYGIOS | minor | minor | 138.56 | Weak |
| HYPOPHRYGIOS | **MAJOR** | minor | 140.11 | Weakest |

### Root Position Availability
All modes: **3/7 triads** in root position (strings 1,3,5 / 2,4,6 / 3,5,7)

### Key Insight: Voice Leading Dominates
The **V-vi deceptive cadence** ranks exceptionally strong (98-104 complexity) in multiple modes due to superior voice leading on the lyre, often outranking even V-I!

This reflects the instrument's physical reality: smooth voice leading can override traditional functional expectations.

## Recommendations by Purpose

### For Western Tonal Music
**Use:** LYDIOS or HYPOLYDIOS
- Major I and V chords
- Strong V-I resolutions available
- Full major scale harmony

### For Natural Minor
**Use:** HYPODORIOS
- Familiar Aeolian/natural minor
- All minor scale harmonies available
- Softer, modal minor quality

### For Modal/Folk Music
**Use:** PHRYGIOS or HYPOPHRYGIOS
- Clear modal character
- Avoid strong functional resolutions
- Folk-like, open sound

### For Experimental/Unusual Sounds
**Use:** DORIOS or MIXOLYDIOS
- Diminished chords in prominent positions
- Unstable, unresolved qualities
- Unique harmonic palette

### For Strongest Cadences
**Use:** MIXOLYDIOS
- Paradoxically has strongest V-I despite dim tonic
- Most efficient voice leading
- Lowest overall progression complexity
