# Ancient Greek Lyre Mode Characteristics Summary
## Based on Just Intonation Chord Progression Analysis

**IMPORTANT:** This analysis uses **JUST INTONATION** (pure integer frequency ratios).
All complexity scores reflect the actual acoustics of a just-tuned 7-string lyre.

**Last Updated:** 2025-10-20 (Version 3 - Just Intonation)

---

## DORIOS (H-W-W-W-H-W-W)
*Ancient Dorios - Modern Phrygian (E-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** E F G A B C D
**Semitones:** [0, 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10]
**Tonal Center:** Minor (i)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is DIMINISHED (v°)** - This creates unique harmonic challenges
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (i, II, III)
- The diminished V chord gives progressions an unstable, modal quality
- Plagal cadence (IV-I) is actually STRONGER than authentic (V-I) due to v° dissonance

### Best Progressions (Just Intonation)
1. **iv - i** (IV-I Plagal): 39.64 - STRONGEST (better than V-I!)
2. **v° - i** (V-I Authentic): 68.93 - Weak due to diminished V
3. **i - v°** (I-V Half): 70.13 - Creates tension
4. **v° - VI** (V-vi Deceptive): 73.98 - Also weak

### Musical Character
- Dark, mysterious quality due to minor tonic and diminished dominant
- Well-suited for modal harmony rather than traditional tonal progressions
- The diminished V prevents strong authentic cadences
- **Plagal cadence becomes primary resolution** due to V° weakness
- Excellent for music that avoids strong dominant-tonic resolutions

### Practical Notes
- **Challenge:** Diminished V chord adds ~30 points of complexity vs normal V
- **Workaround:** Use IV-I (plagal) as primary cadence instead of V-I
- **Best for:** Modal compositions, atmospheric music, avoiding functional harmony
- **Avoid:** Traditional tonal progressions expecting strong V-I resolutions

## PHRYGIOS (W-H-W-W-W-H-W)
*Ancient Phrygios - Modern Dorian (D-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** D E F G A B C
**Semitones:** [0, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 10]
**Tonal Center:** Minor (i)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is MINOR (v)** - Weak leading tone resolution
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (i, ii, III)
- Minor V creates modal rather than tonal sound
- V-I cadence complexity: 16.91 (good for modal music)

### Best Progressions (Just Intonation)
1. **v - i** (V-I Authentic): 16.91 - STRONGEST cadence ✓
2. **i - v** (I-V Half): 18.11 - Consistent 1.2 difference
3. **v - vi°** (V-vi Deceptive): 66.71 - Weaker option
4. **IV - i** (IV-I Plagal): 71.40 - Weakest major cadence

### Musical Character
- Bright minor sound with major IV chord available
- The minor V softens cadential resolution
- Creates a folk-like, modal atmosphere
- The Dorian major VI adds characteristic brightness
- Natural for modal minor progressions

### Practical Notes
- **Strength:** V-I works well despite minor V (complexity only 16.91)
- **Strength:** Major IV and VI chords available for color
- **Best for:** Folk music, modal compositions, jazz modal harmony
- **Character:** Brighter than natural minor, softer resolutions than major

## LYDIOS (W-W-H-W-W-W-H)
*Ancient Lydios - Modern Ionian/Major (C-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** C D E F G A B
**Semitones:** [0, 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11]
**Tonal Center:** MAJOR (I)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is MAJOR (V)** - Strong leading tone! ✓
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (I, ii, iii)
- Full major scale harmony available
- V-I cadence complexity: 15.71 - **LOWEST OF ALL MODES** 🏆

### Best Progressions (Just Intonation)
1. **V - I** (Authentic): 15.71 - **PERFECT!** Lowest complexity 🏆
2. **I - V** (Half): 16.91 - Consistent 1.2 difference ✓
3. **V - vi** (Deceptive): 65.52 - Good voice leading
4. **IV - I** (Plagal): 71.40 - Traditional Amen cadence

### Musical Character
- **THE BEST MODE FOR TRADITIONAL TONAL HARMONY**
- Major tonic with major dominant enables strongest progressions
- All major scale chord progressions available
- Perfect for Western common practice harmony
- The natural 7th (leading tone) creates strong resolution

### Practical Notes
- **Strength:** Absolute best V-I resolution (15.71 complexity)
- **Strength:** Most versatile for Western tonal music
- **Strength:** ii-V-I jazz progressions work perfectly (complexity: 21.08)
- **Best for:** Tonal music, major key compositions, traditional harmony
- **HIGHLY RECOMMENDED** for any traditional Western music on lyre

## MIXOLYDIOS (H-W-W-H-W-W-W)
*Ancient Mixolydios - Modern Locrian (B-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** B C D E F G A
**Semitones:** [0, 1, 3, 5, 6, 8, 10]
**Tonal Center:** Diminished (i°)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **TONIC IS DIMINISHED (i°)** - Highly unusual and problematic!
- **V chord is MAJOR (V)** - But resolves to diminished tonic
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (i°, II, iii)
- V-I cadence complexity: 106.05 - **WORST OF ALL MODES**

### Best Progressions (Just Intonation)
1. **V - VI** (Deceptive): 75.25 - Best available resolution
2. **iv - i°** (Plagal): 75.49 - Almost as good
3. **V - i°** (Authentic): 106.05 - Very weak due to dim tonic
4. **i° - V** (Half): 107.25 - Equally problematic

### Musical Character
- **MOST UNUSUAL MODE** - Diminished tonic is extremely rare
- Despite major V, resolution is fundamentally weak
- Creates highly unstable, unresolved sound
- The diminished tonic provides no sense of rest
- Best used for experimental or transitional passages

### Practical Notes
- **Challenge:** Diminished tonic makes ALL cadences weak (75-107 complexity)
- **Workaround:** Use deceptive or plagal cadences, avoid resolving to i°
- **Unique:** Interesting for creating unresolved, floating harmony
- **Best for:** Experimental music, creating tension, avoiding resolution
- **AVOID** for traditional functional harmony - worst mode for this purpose

## HYPODORIOS (W-H-W-W-H-W-W)
*Ancient Hypodorios - Modern Aeolian/Natural Minor (A-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** A B C D E F G
**Semitones:** [0, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10]
**Tonal Center:** Minor (i)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is MINOR (v)** - Natural minor's characteristic
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (i, ii°, III)
- Classic natural minor harmony
- V-I cadence complexity: 16.91 (good for modal minor)

### Best Progressions (Just Intonation)
1. **v - i** (Authentic): 16.91 - STRONGEST ✓
2. **i - v** (Half): 18.11 - Consistent 1.2 difference
3. **v - VI** (Deceptive): 21.96 - Surprisingly strong!
4. **iv - i** (Plagal): 39.64 - Moderate strength

### Musical Character
- **PURE NATURAL MINOR** - The familiar minor scale sound
- Lacks strong V-I resolution (no raised 7th)
- All progressions have moderate complexity (17-40 range)
- The flatted 7th creates softer, more modal resolutions
- Traditional minor sound without harmonic minor's tension

### Practical Notes
- **Strength:** Clean, familiar natural minor sound
- **Strength:** V-vi (deceptive) is surprisingly strong at 21.96
- **Note:** All cadences relatively similar strength (more democratic)
- **Best for:** Natural minor compositions, modal minor, folk music
- **Character:** Softer than harmonic minor, less directed than major

## HYPOLYDIOS (W-W-W-H-W-W-H)
*Ancient Hypolydios - Modern Lydian (F-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** F G A B C D E
**Semitones:** [0, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9, 11]
**Tonal Center:** MAJOR (I)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is MAJOR (V)** - Strong leading tone! ✓
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (I, II, iii)
- Major scale with raised 4th (Lydian characteristic)
- V-I cadence complexity: 15.71 - **TIED FOR LOWEST** 🏆

### Best Progressions (Just Intonation)
1. **V - I** (Authentic): 15.71 - **PERFECT!** Tied for lowest 🏆
2. **I - V** (Half): 16.91 - Consistent 1.2 difference ✓
3. **V - vi** (Deceptive): 65.52 - Good voice leading
4. **iv° - I** (Plagal): 70.93 - Note: IV is diminished

### Musical Character
- **EXCELLENT FOR TONAL HARMONY** - Tied for best with LYDIOS
- Major tonic and major dominant enable strongest progressions
- The raised 4th creates a brighter, more open sound than Ionian
- Identical progression strengths to LYDIOS mode
- Natural for bright, uplifting major compositions

### Practical Notes
- **Strength:** Perfect V-I like LYDIOS (15.71 complexity)
- **Strength:** V-vi has excellent voice leading (65.52)
- **Unique:** Raised 4th (tritone from root) creates distinctive bright color
- **Best for:** Major key music with a bright, open character
- **HIGHLY RECOMMENDED** - tied for best mode for tonal harmony

## HYPOPHRYGIOS (W-W-H-W-W-H-W)
*Ancient Hypophrygios - Modern Mixolydian (G-mode)*

**Scale Pattern:** G A B C D E F
**Semitones:** [0, 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 10]
**Tonal Center:** MAJOR (I)
**Available Chord Qualities:** 3 major, 3 minor, 1 diminished

### Harmonic Characteristics
- **V chord is MINOR (v)** - Mixolydian's characteristic b7
- Only 3/7 triads available in root position (I, ii, iii°)
- Major tonic but minor dominant (no leading tone)
- V-I cadence complexity: 16.91 (still good for modal major)

### Best Progressions (Just Intonation)
1. **v - I** (Authentic): 16.91 - STRONGEST ✓
2. **I - v** (Half): 18.11 - Consistent 1.2 difference
3. **v - vi** (Deceptive): 66.71 - Moderate option
4. **IV - I** (Plagal): 71.40 - Traditional option

### Musical Character
- Major tonic with flatted 7th (F natural in G)
- Minor V prevents strong dominant function
- Creates a relaxed, folk-like major sound
- The b7 creates a modal rather than tonal character
- Natural for rock, folk, and modal major music

### Practical Notes
- **Character:** Relaxed major sound without sharp leading tone
- **Strength:** V-I still works well despite minor v (16.91 complexity)
- **Unique:** Mixolydian b7 creates distinctive rock/folk character
- **Best for:** Modal major music, folk, rock, Celtic music
- **Good alternative** to pure major when softer resolution desired

## Recommendations by Purpose

### For Western Tonal Music
**Use:** LYDIOS or HYPOLYDIOS
- Perfect V-I resolution (15.71 complexity)
- Major I and V chords
- Strong V-I resolutions available
- Full major scale harmony
- **Best choice for traditional classical/popular music**

### For Natural Minor
**Use:** HYPODORIOS
- Familiar Aeolian/natural minor (16.91 complexity)
- All minor scale harmonies available
- Softer, modal minor quality
- Good for folk, classical minor, modal jazz

### For Modal/Folk Music (Minor)
**Use:** PHRYGIOS
- Bright Dorian sound (16.91 complexity)
- Major VI chord adds brightness
- Clear modal character
- Excellent for folk, Celtic, jazz modal

### For Modal/Folk Music (Major)
**Use:** HYPOPHRYGIOS
- Mixolydian major with b7 (16.91 complexity)
- Relaxed major sound
- Folk-like, open quality
- Perfect for rock, folk, Celtic, bluegrass

### For Experimental/Unusual Sounds
**Use with caution:** DORIOS
- Diminished V creates tension (68.93 complexity)
- Use plagal cadences instead of authentic
- Dark Phrygian character
- Good for modal music avoiding resolution

**Avoid:** MIXOLYDIOS
- Diminished tonic (106.05 complexity)
- No stable resolution possible
- Highly unstable, unresolved
- Only use for experimental/transitional passages

### For Strongest Cadences Overall
**Best modes by V-I strength:**
1. LYDIOS & HYPOLYDIOS: 15.71 (major I, major V)
2. PHRYGIOS, HYPODORIOS, HYPOPHRYGIOS: 16.91 (minor v works well)
3. DORIOS: 68.93 (diminished v° problematic)
4. MIXOLYDIOS: 106.05 (diminished i° - avoid)

### For Circle of Fifths Progressions (ii-V-I)
**Best modes:**
- LYDIOS: 21.08 complexity ✓
- PHRYGIOS: 22.27 complexity ✓
- HYPOPHRYGIOS: 22.27 complexity ✓

All work beautifully for jazz and classical harmony.

## Technical Notes

### Just Intonation Ratios Used
- Major triad: 4:5:6 (perfectly consonant)
- Minor triad: 10:12:15 (consonant)
- Diminished triad: More complex ratios (inherently dissonant)

### Complexity Formula Factors
1. Individual chord consonance (pure integer ratios)
2. Voice leading smoothness (string distances)
3. Root movement strength (V→I = -1.5 bonus)
4. Voicing quality (root position vs inversions)
